1-Bit Editor

Draw 1-bit icons, sprites and screens for Elektron Model:Cycles and Model:Samples mods. A pixel is dark or light; the patterns stand in for gray and line up across the whole sprite.

Click a swatch in the Colors panel to draw with it on the left mouse button, right-click it for the right one. L and R mark the two choices.

What you draw is what the device shows: light pixels are lit, dark ones stay off. The canvas under all layers is light, a lit screen; for a dark screen like the OS's own, fill a layer with Dark. The left button starts out with Dark, the right one with Light. Transparent erases, so what lies below shows through. Moved or deleted pixels and new canvas area are transparent too.

Layers

Upper layers cover the ones below, and under all of them the canvas is lit. The tools draw on the selected layer; its LED is lit. Shift-click adds a layer to the selection or takes it out.

Drag a layer to move it. Drop it onto another layer to group the two, or onto a group to put it inside. Double-click a name to rename it; the eye hides a layer. Hidden layers are left out of what you export, but the PNG keeps them.

The Move tool shifts the selected layers on the canvas. What ends up outside the canvas is cut off once the move is done; while you drag or nudge, nothing is lost yet.

Text

The Text tool makes text layers: click to put one there and type, Enter finishes. Click a text to edit it, drag it to move it; the options set its font, alignment and style. A text layer stays text: the .bin leaves it out, because the device draws text in its own fonts, the PNGs show it in the editor's stand-in fonts, and the .json beside the .bin says what it says and where. To pixels turns it into an ordinary layer, for a label that belongs in the sprite.

Stamps

Drag a stamp from the Stamps panel onto the canvas, or click it to put it in the middle. It floats on the selected layer like pasted pixels: drag it or nudge it with the arrow keys, then press Enter or click next to it. Delete throws it away. Stamps bring their own dark and lit pixels; where they are transparent, what lies below stays.

To keep your own, select an area and press Save selection under Custom. Your stamps stay in this browser.

Layout grid

The grid button left of the size folds out the layout grid: columns and rows laid over the canvas to count by. For each you set how many, the gap between them and the margin outside them, in pixels; what doesn't divide evenly goes to the first ones, a pixel each. Thin red lines show their edges; they are never part of the pixels, and Layout at the bottom hides them. The status line names the column and row under the pointer. The grid is saved with the sprite and in its PNG, and the .json lists it.

Guides and rulers

Rulers run along the top and the left of the canvas; y counts as the status line does. Drag down out of the top ruler for a guide across, or right out of the left one for a guide down. A guide lies on the edge between two pixels and shows in cyan; while you drag it, the ruler and the status line show where it lands. Move it with the Move tool, and drag it off the canvas to remove it. Guides and Rulers at the bottom hide them. Guides are saved like the layout grid, and the .json lists them.

Slices

A slice is a box over the screen that comes out as a sprite of its own: draw a whole screen in place, then mark the icons in it. The Slice tool drags one out; drag a slice to move it, its corners to resize it, and Delete removes it. Its edges snap to the canvas's, the guides, the layout grid and the other slices. From selection and From layer in the Slices panel make one around the selection or around what the selected layer holds; double-click a name there to rename it. Slices show in green and are never part of the pixels; Slices at the bottom hides them. The .bin button then saves each slice's .bin and PNG beside the whole screen's, and the .json lists their boxes in the OS's coordinates. Slices are saved with the sprite and in its PNG.

Frames

An animation is a list of frames, each with layers of its own; the Frames panel shows them in a strip. Add puts an empty frame after the current one, Duplicate a copy of it (its layers keep their names, so the selection carries over), Delete removes it; drag a frame to reorder, , and . step through them. Onion skin shows the frame before faintly. The Drive is what moves the animation: with Time each frame has its milliseconds and Play runs them in the On screen panel; with Value a value from 0 to 127 picks the frame, the way a knob's picture changes on the device: the frames are spread evenly over From to To (0 to 127 by default), and the slider's value chooses the frame to edit. With as many frames as values, each value has its own frame.

Files: the PNGs hold the frames side by side and open again as the animation; the .bin button's ZIP holds one .bin per frame (included one after the other they form one table), the strip PNG, each slice's bins and strip, and in the .json an animation block with the drive, the ms (and UI ticks) per frame or the value map; GIF saves an animated GIF, 4× bigger, for sharing.

Panels

Drag a panel by its title to move it. Drop it on the middle of another panel to share that place as tabs, and drag a tab out to give it its own place again. The layout stays in this browser.

Files

Save PNG writes the working file: 1 bit, as the device shows it (white = light = lit), with the layers inside. Open it again to keep drawing, layers and all; other programs just see the picture. PNG ×8 is a bigger copy for sharing; it opens again at its real size. An animation's frames lie side by side in it.

Open takes a PNG, a .bin, a .json or the ZIP the .bin button saves; pick or drop several at once. A .bin holds no texts, so the editor asks for its .json, which brings them back as text layers. A .json on its own adds its texts to the canvas, if the size fits.

The .bin button saves a .zip with the .bin and a .json that describes the screen for whoever builds it into a mod, a person or an agent: the texts with their anchors, fonts and styles, every layer with the box it covers, and the slices, in the OS's coordinates (y from the bottom; OS y in the status line counts the same way). With slices, the ZIP also holds each slice's .bin and PNG.

.bin holds the pixels of the visible layers, without text, the way the OS stores its own sprites: column after column, ⌈height ÷ 32⌉ big-endian 32-bit words per column, and bit 7 of the first byte is the bottom-left pixel, because the OS counts y from the bottom. A 48 × 33 sprite is 384 bytes. A mod includes it with .incbin "icon_48x33.bin". The size in the file name lets the editor open it again.
